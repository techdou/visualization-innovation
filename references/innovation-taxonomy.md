# Visualization Innovation Taxonomy

## Encoding
New mappings, difference-first views, uncertainty-integrated marks, topology-aware encoding, missingness/absence encoding, composite glyphs.

## Layout
Anchors, force/constraint, contain-and-fill, task-driven ordering, semantic neighborhoods, focus+context, hierarchical nesting, multiscale layouts.

## Interaction
Direct manipulation, semantic brushing, lenses, parameter steering, compositional instruments, feedback-rich actions, reversible and branchable transformations.

## Coordination
Innovation between views: shared state, cross-scale transitions, parameter↔result coupling, disagreement↔evidence coupling, overview↔raw evidence.

## Panel composition
Functional asymmetry, focal views, evidence satellites, comparison corridors, provenance lanes, nested detail, cross-scale stacks.

## Narrative
Guided sequence, reader branches, callout-anchored facts, interaction-derived steps, temporal view shifts, storylines, bidirectional text-visual grounding.

## Authoring
Direct manipulation, design-space exploration, demonstration-to-story, blended UI+NL, reusable grammars, mixed-initiative suggestions with editable history.

## Medium
Document, word-scale, video, AR/VR, multi-display, tangible, spatial/physical context.

## Workflow
Discovery→curation→explanation, anomaly→evidence→counterfactual, steering→recomputation→explanation, authoring→preview→revise→compare branches.

## Diagnostic ladder
- Level 0: styling only.
- Level 1: configuration of a known chart.
- Level 2: purposeful composition of known primitives.
- Level 3: new encoding/layout/interaction/coordination mechanism.
- Level 4: new workflow/authoring/medium model.

Higher is not automatically better; task fit and evidence gain dominate.
