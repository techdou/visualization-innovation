# Academic Foundations for Visualization Innovation

## Nested Model
Munzner, *A Nested Model for Visualization Design and Validation*, IEEE TVCG, 2009. Innovation can occur at domain, data/task, encoding/interaction, or algorithm levels; novelty at the wrong level does not solve the problem.

## Design Study Methodology
Sedlmair, Meyer, Munzner, *Design Study Methodology: Reflections from the Trenches and the Stacks*, IEEE TVCG, 2012, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2012.213. Novel visuals should emerge from real analytical difficulty and end with a validation hypothesis.

## Design Activity Framework
McKenna et al., *Design Activity Framework for Visualization Design*, IEEE TVCG, 2014, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2014.2346331. Understanding, ideating, making, and deploying overlap; prototypes may change the framing.

## Five Design-Sheet Methodology
Roberts, Headleand, Ritsos, *Sketching Designs Using the Five Design-Sheet Methodology*, IEEE TVCG, 2016, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2015.2467271. The key lesson for agents is divergence before convergence.

## Design by Immersion
Hall et al., *Design by Immersion: A Transdisciplinary Approach to Problem-Driven Visualizations*, IEEE TVCG, 2020, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2019.2934790. Domain practices can expose domain-specific metaphors, constraints, actions, and entirely new design spaces.

## Narrative Visualization
Segel & Heer, *Narrative Visualization: Telling Stories with Data*, IEEE TVCG, 2010, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2010.179. Story design balances author-driven messaging and reader-driven exploration.

## Automation in narrative authoring
Chen et al., *How Does Automation Shape the Process of Narrative Visualization: A Survey of Tools*, IEEE TVCG 30(8), 2024, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3261320. Distinguish narrative genre, authoring stage, and automation level; creative control should not collapse into one-shot prompting.
